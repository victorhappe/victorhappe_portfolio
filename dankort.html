Campaign film

Ønskebrønden

The story

The film follows two young women travelling by train to Klampenborg Station before walking together into Dyrehaven.

Along the way, the camera focuses on their friendship, the natural surroundings and the small moments they share. The story ends with the two friends enjoying a picnic in the sunlight, surrounded by Danish nature.

The message

The film communicates that Dankort is part of the everyday moments that create experiences and memories.

Dankort is integrated naturally through subtle close-up shots of the card being used as a payment method. This connects the product to friendship, shared experiences and the importance of protecting the places around us.

“Some places are worth taking care of.”

Creative direction

An authentic early-2000s aesthetic

The visual style was inspired by authentic commercials from the early 2000s. We wanted the film to feel personal, nostalgic and believable rather than overly polished or traditional.

Handheld camerawork, soft movements, close-up shots and wide landscape images were used to highlight the atmosphere and details of Dyrehaven.

Natural lighting and warm colour tones helped create a summery, intimate and down-to-earth expression.

My contribution

  • Edited the film with a focus on rhythm, atmosphere and narrative flow

About the campaign

A story about nature and shared experiences

The campaign was developed for Dankort Earmark and aimed at young adults aged 18-28.

The overall concept focused on Danish nature, friendship, community and appreciating the places around us. The campaign combined the film with a long-scroll website and social media content.

Rather than explaining Dankort Earmark through statistics, we wanted to communicate the idea through a relatable experience and a warm, emotional story.

What I learned

Using film to communicate a campaign message

Through this project, I strengthened my skills in video production, editing and visual storytelling. I also learned how product placement, sound, pacing, camera movement and colour can support a message without making the film feel like a traditional advertisement.
